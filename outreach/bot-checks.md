# Bot checks

Before calling a company, open its public support chat and ask 3-5 ordinary customer questions,
the way a real customer would. It takes ten minutes, it tells you what they already have (so you
do not pitch a bot to someone who runs one), and it gives you something specific to say on the
call. Be a normal customer: a few questions, no stress-testing, no tricking it, no repeated
sessions. Do not enter real personal or payment details.

Questions worth asking: "refund policy", "how do I cancel", "do you have an uptime guarantee",
"can I talk to a person", and one specific question about their own product.

## Reviews check

Customer reviews tell you what a chat session will not. On the review sites, open the company's
product page, sort by newest, and read the cons plus any review that mentions "support", "help"
or "response". Note a short quote, the date, the rating and the link. Treat one review as a
lead to test, not a finding: it is one person, often about the product rather than your target
area, and sometimes about a plan or period that has changed. Three or more recent reviews saying
the same thing are a pattern.

## What to record

```
Company:
Date:
Widget and AI agent (name shown, "AI agent" label, vendor if visible):
Questions asked and the answer in your own words:
Did it cite sources? How (numbered badges, links)?
Did it ask a follow-up or collect details?
How does it end the answer? (e.g. "Is that what you were looking for?")
How easy was it to reach a person? How many steps?
What it did well:
What it got wrong, left out, or could not do:
What I would ask them about it on a call:
```

## Entries

### Vidyard (screenshot, 2026-10-12)

- **Widget:** chat messenger headed "VY", with "The team can also help". Same layout as Intercom's
  messenger. I cannot tell from the screenshot which product powers it. Confirm.
- **Asked:** "refund policy".
- **Answer:** called the policy "generally strict" and stated that paid subscription fees are
  non-cancellable and non-refundable, including when you cancel or do not use the service. It added
  possible exceptions (Vidyard terminates without cause, or a confirmed material breach by
  Vidyard) and said you may be asked for card last four digits, charge date, amount and
  cardholder name to locate a transaction.
- **Citations:** two numbered badges on the answer.
- **Observations:** a confident, specific policy answer drawn from contract-style terms, which is
  exactly the kind of answer that creates liability if wrong. Wording ("generally strict", "may
  be refunded") reads like terms of service, not customer-friendly. It prepares the customer to
  hand over payment details in chat.
- **Ask on a call:** how often does it get policy questions wrong or need a person to step in?
  Who checks its answers on contract terms?

### Gloo (screenshot, 2026-10-12)

- **Widget:** header "Fin", labelled "AI Agent", "The team can also help". Fin's own label, so
  this one is Intercom's Fin.
- **Asked:** "refund policy".
- **Answer:** broke the policy down by product type: digital products non-refundable once
  purchased, subscriptions non-refundable but can be cancelled any time with access until the
  end of the billing period, messaging credit packs non-refundable, physical products follow the
  fulfilment partner's return policy. Four numbered citations. Asked which subscription and which
  account email, to guide the person further.
- **Closer:** "Is that what you were looking for?" after the answer.
- **Observations:** organised well, and it moves toward helping with a specific account. The
  closing question is the mechanism behind Fin's "resolution": a customer who confirms or leaves
  can count as resolved and be billed (see `INDUSTRY-TRUTHS-TODO.md`, Truth 3). Worth noting, not
  proving: I only saw one answer.

### Vidyard: review signal (screenshot, 2026-10-12)

- A review dated February 2025 (4.0 stars, an independent creator) lists as its one con:
  "Sometimes the support is difficult to contact."
- **How strong:** weak. One reviewer, about reaching support in general, not necessarily the
  chat, and the chat may be newer than the review. Check for more recent reviews before leaning on
  it.
- **Why it still matters:** difficulty reaching a person is the problem Nexo's "every answer has an
  exit" idea targets. It could also be a deliberate choice, since vendors sometimes make humans
  harder to reach to push self-service and cut cost, and that company may not want it fixed.
- **Test on a call (neutral, no quoting the review):** "When a customer needs a person, how do
  they reach one?" and "How often does that happen?"
