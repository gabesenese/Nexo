# Nexo — CEO Playbook

> Written 2026-10-10 for a first-time founder who is new to the AI customer-support industry.
> It is an operating manual, not a strategy: strategy lives in `PHILOSOPHY.md`, evidence in
> `COMPETITORS.md`, `MARKET-VALIDATION.md` and `INDUSTRY-TRUTHS-TODO.md`, risks in
> `REALITY-CHECK.md`. This file says how to *run the company week to week*. Edit it as you learn
> what works; a playbook nobody changes is a playbook nobody uses.
>
> This is not legal, tax or financial advice. Use a lawyer and an accountant for incorporation,
> trademark, contracts and taxes.

---

## 1. What the job is right now

For the next 90 days you are the **chief learning officer** of a company that has a product and no
proof of demand. The product is ahead of the business. Your two scarce resources are **time** and
**runway**, and the CEO's job is to point both at the biggest unknown: will a real buyer pay?

Three rules follow:

1. **Customer contact beats building.** If a task does not produce a conversation, a pilot, a
   measurement or a decision, it waits.
2. **Learn the industry from the people in it.** You are new, so borrow expertise instead of pretending.
3. **Tell yourself the truth first.** Write down what you expect, then check it. Reward bad news.

## 2. How to spend your time (heuristic, adjust with data)

| Share of week | What | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| About 50% | Buyer conversations, follow-ups, audits, pilots | The only way to learn if the company works |
| About 30% | Product: only items traceable to a customer need or a P0 task | Keeps you shippable without hiding in code |
| About 20% | Legal, money, hosting, admin | Needed, but should not grow |

Check it every Friday. If product is above 50% for two weeks running, that is the signal to stop and
book calls. If you do not know the split, track it for one week; most founders are surprised.

## 3. Weekly operating rhythm

**Monday (30 min): set the week.**
- Read last week's numbers (section 6) and the decision log (section 5).
- Pick **three outcomes**. At least one must be a customer conversation, pilot or audit.
- Write them at the top of `weekly-log.md` (template in section 8).

**Every working day:**
- Two customer touches: an interview, a follow-up, an outreach message or an audit.
- One thing shipped or decided. Not "worked on": shipped or decided.

**Friday (45 min): review.**
- Update the one-page numbers.
- Add to the decision log and the learning log.
- Answer: *What did I learn about the industry this week? What did I avoid? What do I do differently next week?*

**Monthly (2 hours): step back.**
- Re-read `REALITY-CHECK.md` and `INDUSTRY-TRUTHS-TODO.md`. Which P0s are done? Which assumptions
  broke?
- Talk to a mentor or another founder. Say the uncomfortable thing out loud.
- Decide: continue, change, or stop (section 7).

## 4. Learning the industry when you are new

**Get three kinds of people in your life:**
1. **Practitioners**: heads of support or support managers at small SaaS companies. They tell you
   what the work is really like. Aim for 3 who will take a call every month.
2. **An advisor** who has run or sold support software. Ask one, with a simple written advisor
   agreement (have a lawyer draft it before offering anything of value).
3. **A peer founder** one stage ahead. They tell you what the next 6 months feel like.

**Fast ways to learn the work:**
- Ask a friendly support team if you can watch them handle tickets for two hours.
- Read 100 real tickets (with permission) and tag them: answerable from docs, needs account data,
  needs judgement.
- Sit through the setup of two competitors' products as a customer would, and write down every
  moment of confusion.
- Keep a **glossary** of every term you did not know (deflection, CSAT, first-contact resolution,
  handle time). If you cannot explain a term to a friend, you do not know it yet.

**Where newcomers get hurt:** copying a number from a blog post into a pitch. Every figure in front of
a buyer should be either one you measured or one with a primary source you have read.

## 5. Decision log

Decisions get made faster and reversed less when you write down the reason. One entry per decision
that costs more than a day or more than C$100, or that is hard to undo.

```
Date:
Decision:
Why (evidence, not feelings):
What I expect to happen, by when:
What would change my mind:
Reversible? (easy / costly / no)
```

**Reversible decisions** (copy changes, a price test, which prospect to call): decide in minutes.
**Costly or one-way decisions** (name, incorporation, taking money, a long contract, hiring): slow
down, sleep on it, talk to one person who has done it.

Review old entries monthly. The point is to learn how good your judgement is and where it fails.

## 6. The one-page numbers

Update every Friday. A blank cell is information: it means you do not measure it yet.

| Metric | This week | Last week | Target / note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Runway** (months of cash and your own time) | | | Know this first; set a decision date |
| Buyer conversations held | | | 20 in the first 6 weeks |
| Conversations that described the pain with numbers | | | H1 in `MARKET-VALIDATION.md` |
| Docs audits delivered | | | 5 before any pilot decision |
| Pilots offered / signed (paid) | | | 5 signed = go; fewer = change something |
| Cost per conversation (C$) | | | Not instrumented yet; P0 |
| Gross margin on the cheapest plan | | | Depends on the line above |
| Resolution rate on real customer data | | | Not measured yet |
| Hours on customers / product / admin | | | See section 2 |

Do not add vanity metrics (page views, followers, signups from friends). A metric belongs here only
if it can change a decision.

## 7. Money, runway and decision dates

**Know your runway in months.** Add up cash you can spend on this, divide by monthly spend, and
include your own living costs if you are not paid. Write the number at the top of the numbers page.

**Set three dates now and write them down:**
1. **Day 30:** Have you held 10 buyer conversations? If not, why not?
2. **Day 90:** The go / change / stop decision (kill criteria in `MARKET-VALIDATION.md`).
3. **The last day** you can keep going without income. Decide it in a calm week, not a panic one.

**Bootstrap vs raise.** Hosting is about C$5 a month, so you can reach first revenue without outside
money. Raising before you have evidence usually means giving up ownership for a story. Revisit when
you have paying pilots and a repeatable way of finding more. Get advice from a lawyer before taking
any money or promising any equity.

**Spend rule:** nothing over C$100 without an entry in the decision log.

## 9. How to sell when you are new

- **Say you are early.** "We are a new product and I want to learn how you handle support. Can I
  run your help center through it for free and show you the result?" Honesty is a better opener than
  claimed expertise.
- **Ask about the past, not the future.** "Tell me about the last time..." beats "Would you use...".
- **Sell the next step, not the product.** The goal of each call is the next call, an audit or a pilot.
- **Do not discount your way to a yes.** Ask for the paid pilot at a fair price; "free" teaches
  buyers the product is worth nothing, and a pilot nobody pays for proves nothing.
- **Never overclaim.** Do not quote the 66% figure, residency or speed until they are true and you
  can show them. One caught exaggeration costs a customer and a reference.
- **Do not hire a salesperson until you have personally closed your first ten customers.** You
  cannot manage or coach a process you have not run.

## 10. Traps for a first-time founder

| Trap | What it looks like | What to do instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Building to avoid selling | Another feature, another redesign | Book a call first, then build |
| Writing strategy instead of testing it | A new vision document | Write a hypothesis and a date to test it |
| Trusting polished research | Quoting a vendor blog as fact | Check primary sources; ask buyers |
| Seeking approval | Showing the product to friends who say it is great | Show it to people who would have to pay |
| Hiring or raising to feel progress | A contractor, a deck | Wait for a repeatable pattern |
| Perfecting before shipping | Weeks on polish | Ship the smallest thing that answers the question |
| Solo burnout | Seven-day weeks, no days off | Fixed hours, one real day off, a monthly check-in |

## 11. Staying sane as a solo founder

- Set working hours and a real day off. A rested founder makes better decisions than a tired one.
- Find one person you can tell the truth to about how it is going.
- Celebrate evidence, not effort: a "no" with a reason is progress.
- When a week goes badly, write it in the log and move on. The log, not your memory, decides
  whether the company is working.

## 12. The first 14 days, concretely

| Day | Do |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Write your runway number and the three dates (section 7). Create the weekly log. |
| 2 | Make a list of 40 SaaS companies with 2-20 support staff you could contact. |
| 3 | Write one outreach message (offer a free docs audit). Send 10. |
| 4 | Start the trademark search for the name; check domain availability. |
| 5 | Friday review: numbers page, decision log, learning log. |
| 6-7 | Rest. |
| 8 | Hold your first 2-3 conversations. Use the script in `MARKET-VALIDATION.md`. |
| 9 | Cost tracking is built. Ask Nexo five questions on your own machine, then run `npm run cost:report --workspace=@nexo/server` and read what a conversation costs. |
| 10 | Send 10 more outreach messages. Book one practitioner and one advisor call. |
| 11 | Run the Plausible head-to-head questions through Nexo once an AI key is available. |
| 12 | Hold 2-3 more conversations. |
| 13 | Rewrite any public claim you cannot prove (residency, 66%). |
| 14 | Friday review. Compare with day 1. What changed? What did you avoid? |

## What this playbook does not cover

Incorporation, taxes, hiring, equity, fundraising mechanics and customer contracts. Each needs a
professional, and each can wait until there is something to protect. If you tell me your runway,
hours per week and whether you have a co-founder or mentor, this file can be made specific to them.
